Sailing

Moving on the map

A click on a map tile shows the path to it, and a second click on the same tile sends the crew along it. The keys W, A, S and D walk the crew one tile up, left, down and right, and Q, E, Z and C walk it diagonally. The number pad does the same.

Time passes only while your crew travels. When it stands still, the game pauses and other crews stand still too.

On foot and aboard the ship

On foot the crew crosses land tiles only, and aboard the ship it crosses water tiles only.

  • On foot, moving to an open water tile brings up "Summon your ship?". Aye puts the ship on that tile with the crew aboard, wherever the ship was left.
  • Aboard the ship, moving to a land tile puts the crew ashore on that tile once the ship is beside it. The ship stays on its water tile.

Knots, Vision and Navigating

The Map button on the HUD opens the ship screen, which shows the ship's three stats, the Morale meter, the hold and the map.

  • Knots is how swiftly the crew crosses the map. A step takes 700 divided by Knots frames. The crew has 25 Knots on foot, and boarding the ship adds 10.
  • Vision is how far the crew sees, in tiles. It is 5 on foot, and boarding the ship adds 1. Sight runs in straight lines from the crew: cover shortens a line and mountains stop it.
  • Navigating is the share of the rough terrain penalty the crew avoids. It comes from the ship's fittings and from the best Navigator trait among the crewmates.

Rough terrain makes a step onto it take longer. A step onto pack ice takes three times as long as a normal step. A step onto kelp or lily pads at sea, or thickets or marsh on land, takes one and a half times as long.

A tile the crew has seen once stays uncovered for the rest of the run, and a location on it is marked on the map. Another crew is drawn only while it stands on a tile your crew sees at that moment.

Ship fittings

The ship has four slots for fittings: sails, prow, instrument and lamp. Ship upgrades go in them and change the ship's stats: Kelp Sail, for example, adds 5 Knots.

Hazards on the sea

  • Open water holds reefs the ship cannot enter: icebergs in arctic water, rocks in cursed and taiga water, and coral in forest, desert, blessed and swamp water.
  • It also holds patches of slow water: pack ice in arctic and taiga water, lily pads in swamp water, and kelp in forest, desert and blessed water.
  • In the Soul Fog, Vision is 3 lower, or 2 lower with the Dead Man's Lamp fitted to the ship.

Food and rum

Food costs 2 gold a ration wherever it is sold. Each level of the Cook trait lowers the crew's food consumption by 1. A Sea Crate opened while the crew has no food always holds food.

Rum is carried in the ship's hold. Sharing one raises morale by 1, to at most 5, and uses it up.

Morale

Morale is a number from 0 to 5, and a new crew starts at 5. The Morale meter on the HUD shows one bar for each point and a face that changes with it; its tooltip says "Crew morale affects random events, and crewmate traits." A click on the meter offers a Rum from the hold.

  • Fleeing a battle lowers morale by 1, but never below 1.
  • Each day, a crewmate with the Optimist trait has a 20% chance to raise morale by 1 while it is below 5.

Resting

Rest is offered only at a place where one of a faction's heralds lives. It heals every crewmate by 4 Health, or by 2 if the crewmate has the Sick trait, and passes one day at once. That day's rations are eaten straight away.

Rest is free where your reputation with the place's faction is above 0. It costs 5 gold at reputation 0, and is refused below 0.

Other crews

Selecting a crew shows its Might above it and your own Might above your crew. An icon above a crew shows what it is doing: angry while it has a target, fleeing while it runs away, confused after it gives up a chase, and crossed swords between two crews that fight each other.

  • A crew treats yours as hostile when its faction's reputation with you is below 0.
  • A hostile crew attacks when its own Might, plus 10 for each point of reputation below 0, is greater than your Might. Otherwise it flees.
  • A crew aboard a ship does not attack a crew on land, because a crew only goes after one that stands on a tile it can cross itself.
  • A whale never attacks on the map. It runs.
  • A crew gives up a chase after 25 tiles at reputation -1 and 30 tiles at reputation -2, and goes back to where it started.

Moving to a tile that holds another crew opens a window with Nay, Trade if the crew sells, Talk and Attack. See Factions and trade for what Talk and Attack do to your standing.

When a hostile crew that attacks reaches a tile next to yours, a message names the attacker and the battle starts. See Battles.

The storm

A storm comes every 13 days: on day 13, day 26, day 39 and so on. Hovering the hourglass on the HUD shows "Next Storm" with the day of the next one.

What ends a run

A run ends when a battle is lost, which is when nobody on your side is left standing in it.
