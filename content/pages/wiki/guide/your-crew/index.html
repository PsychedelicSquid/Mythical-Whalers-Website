Choosing a starting crew

A starting crew has three crewmates. The crew creation screen has one column for each of five factions: Selkie, Fae, Ravagers, Empire and Immortals. Each open slot in a column offers one candidate of its race.

  • Randomize, the dice, rolls a new portrait and name for the candidate on offer. It never changes the candidate's traits.
  • Rename lets you type a new name, starting from the rolled one.
  • Recruiting seats the candidate, and the slot rolls a new candidate of the same race, so a crew can hold more than one crewmate of a race.
  • Clicking a seated crewmate takes them out of the crew and puts them back on offer.

Start stays greyed out until all three seats are filled. A crewmate chosen here starts at level 1 with nothing equipped.

Unlocking more races

On a first run only the top slot of each column is open. Each slot below it needs one more rank of status with that faction: the second, third and fourth races need status 1, 2 and 3. A locked slot's tooltip reads "Required Status" with the faction and the rank, for example Selkie +1. A faction's page lists its races in the order they unlock.

Races are unlocked when the run ends. The Game Over card compares the status the crew holds with each faction against each locked slot and shows "New crewmate unlocked!" for each race earned. An unlocked race stays open in every later run.

What a race is born with

Every crewmate of a race is born with the same traits, shown as framed icons on the crew creation screen. A Salorian, for example, starts with Cryomancer and Priest. A race's page lists them under STARTS WITH.

Bonds

Every two crewmates share a bond from 0 to 10, the same in both directions. Its tooltip names both crewmates and says they:

  • are the best of friends, at 8 or more;
  • are friendly, at 6 or more;
  • are indifferent towards each other, at 4 or more;
  • get on each other's nerves, at 2 or more;
  • despise each other, below 2.

Two crewmates of the same faction start with a bond of 6. Two crewmates of different factions start at 3 when either faction has a reputation below 0 towards the other. Otherwise they start at 6 if either faction names the other as its ally, and at 5 if not. On the crew creation screen, a badge beside each seated crewmate shows the bond they would start with towards the candidate on offer.

Experience and levels

Experience is earned in battle:

  • A crewmate whose action takes Health from an enemy gains the enemy's Might times the Health taken, divided by the enemy's maximum Health.
  • A crewmate that loses Health to an enemy gains 1 experience for each Health lost.
  • Healing an ally gives the healer 2 experience for each Health restored.
  • An ability that deals no damage gives its user the ability's points for each status effect it newly puts on a target.

A crewmate reaches level 2 at 12 experience, level 3 at 40, level 4 at 75, level 5 at 125, level 6 at 190, level 7 at 275, level 8 at 390 and level 9 at 525. Level 9 is the highest. Level-ups are given out after a won battle.

Level-ups and traits

Each level gained opens a Level Up! window that offers three different traits, and the crewmate learns one of them. Only 22 traits can be offered, and negative traits are never among them. A trait's page says whether it is offered at level-up.

Every race sorts those traits into five tiers, and the tier is the trait's weight in the draw: common 70, uncommon 20, rare 9, legendary 1 and never 0. A race's page lists its tiers under TRAITS OFFERED AT LEVEL-UP.

  • If the crewmate already has an offered trait, the offer is that trait's next level.
  • A trait's highest level is 3 unless the trait sets its own: Hearty can reach level 99, and Steady has only level 1. A trait at its highest level is no longer offered.
  • Four pairs of traits cancel each other: Coward and Heroic, Hearty and Frail, Eagle Eye and Tremor, Optimist and Paranoid. Gaining one while having the other does not add it: the trait the crewmate has drops one level, or is removed at level 1.

Equipment

A crewmate has five equipment slots: head, chest, feet, hand and trinket. Each slot holds one item, and an item goes only into its own slot. A true melee weapon, such as a sword, spear or axe, replaces the crewmate's basic attack. Any other item with an ability, such as a shooting weapon, a shield or a bomb, adds that ability and leaves the basic melee attack in place.

Hiring more crewmates

A location with crewmates for hire shows a recruit button that opens its Tavern. A location's page lists the races for hire there.

  • Each such location offers two crewmates at a time. Hiring one sends the other away, and the Tavern then reads "No crewmates to hire, come back next week".
  • A crewmate for hire comes with a random number of extra levels, from 0 up to the day number divided by 10, rounded down. For each extra level it has already learned one trait.
  • The price is the race's hire cost plus a quarter of that cost for each level above 1, rounded down. A level 3 Mer, whose hire cost is 45, costs 67.
  • A crew that already has 6 crewmates cannot hire another.

Losing a crewmate

A crewmate that reaches 0 Health in battle is knocked down, not killed. See Battles for how one gets back up, and how one is lost for good.

You can also remove a crewmate yourself: at sea the game asks to make them walk the plank, and on land to abandon them ashore. They are gone for good, and the items they had equipped go back to the crew's inventory. The last crewmate cannot be removed.
